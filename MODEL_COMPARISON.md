# Dependence-aware model comparison

GridCast compares a specified model family using weekly MAE differences rather
than treating 8,736 hourly errors as independent observations.

## Protocol

For candidate model A and reference model B, each weekly fold contributes:

```text
improvement = weekly MAE(B) - weekly MAE(A)
```

Positive values favor the candidate. The primary interval uses:

- 52 paired weekly folds with 168 hours each;
- circular block bootstrap with four-week blocks;
- 100,000 resamples generated by NumPy PCG64;
- fixed seed `20260903`;
- percentile 95% marginal intervals;
- all six pairwise comparisons among the four specified models;
- Bonferroni-adjusted 99.17% per-comparison intervals, giving at least 95%
  simultaneous coverage for the six-comparison family;
- sensitivity checks with circular blocks of 2, 4, 6, 8, 13, and 26 weeks, plus a
  four-week moving-block bootstrap.

Generate the artifacts after the benchmark and both TimesFM runs:

```bash
make comparison
```

Outputs are written to `artifacts/model-comparison/` and include weekly losses,
paired comparisons, sensitivity results, source hashes, upstream manifests, and
a digest of the primary resample-index matrix.

“Supported” means the Bonferroni-adjusted interval is entirely above zero,
controlling the simultaneous family error at 5% for the six specified pairs. It
does not establish untouched generalization, production fitness, or a Bayesian
probability that one model is superior.

## Interpretation

- The observed LightGBM advantage over the strong daily baseline is too small
  for 52 dependence-aware folds to resolve.
- TimesFM 2.5's observed advantage over both classical comparators is stable under the fixed
  resampling protocol.
- TimesFM 3 has lower aggregate MAE than TimesFM 2.5, but their paired interval
  crosses zero and all block-length sensitivity intervals reach the same
  qualitative conclusion.
- TimesFM 3's advantage over both classical comparators is supported in this
  sequence, but its
  weights remain non-commercial and unsuitable for production.

## Limitations

- The holdout was repeatedly inspected and is not a confirmatory final test.
- One year supplies only 52 weekly units and one annual cycle.
- Circular blocks introduce a year-end wraparound; moving blocks underweight
  endpoints. Both are reported as sensitivity checks.
- First- and second-half effects differ materially for several pairs, exposing
  nonstationarity that block resampling cannot remove.
- Intervals are conditional on the existing forecasts and exclude training,
  model-selection, checkpoint, and data-preparation uncertainty.
- Foundation-model pretraining overlap with public energy series cannot be ruled
  out.
- Some upstream manifests predate clean-worktree tracking or record dirty runs;
  source file and manifest hashes are retained in the comparison summary.
